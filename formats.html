For makers

Three ways to show the work

Choose how your craft is presented. Whichever you pick, it is framed the same way — generous images, room to breathe, no countdown timers, no confetti. A live session on Provenance should feel closer to a studio visit than a shopping channel.

01

Static listing with process video

The default, and the quietest. A set of large photographs, the maker's card, and a short verified film of the piece being made. It sits still and waits to be found. Best for one-of-a-kind objects and makers who would rather work than broadcast.

Always eligible for all three markers
02

Short-form process clip — AURA Studios

A 15–60 second vertical film of the technique itself, shown full-screen in the AURA Studios feed. Ambient studio audio only — no music overlay, no voiceover, no fast cuts. A translucent card carries your name, region, process badge and price, with an Acquire Piece button on the overlay. No view counts, likes, comments or followers anywhere on it.

Counts toward process-video verification
03

Live selling session

A scheduled broadcast from the workshop, announced a week ahead like a gallery opening. The maker shows pieces, answers questions, and takes acquisitions in real time. Held to the platform's pace: no artificial scarcity, no "three left!", the same calm frame as every other page.

Recording is archived as the listing's process video
The through-line

Same tone, whatever you choose

Provenance does not reward the format that generates the most motion. A live session and a still listing are recommended by the same logic — completed purchases and private 10-star craftsmanship scores — and both are subject to the same ceiling: rotation pauses at 500 daily sales or 50 high-rated feed allocations, whichever comes first.

Switch formats whenever you like. A piece can begin as a live session and settle into a static listing once the broadcast is archived.
