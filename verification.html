Provenance / Verification & fairness
How trust works here

Quiet proof, shared visibility

Authenticity should be verifiable without shouting, and no maker — however good — should be allowed to crowd out the rest. Four mechanisms hold that line, in the gallery and in the AURA Studios feed alike.

One — verification markers

Three marks, deliberately small

A marker is earned, not bought, and reviewed each year. It appears as a faint disc beside a piece or a maker — legible if you look, invisible if you don't. There is no "blue tick" energy here.

  • Process video verified

    A Provenance reviewer has watched unbroken footage of this piece — or this technique — being made by this maker, and matched it to the finished object.

  • Cooperative endorsed

    A recognised guild, cooperative or community body has vouched that the maker belongs to the tradition and works within it. Useful where a craft is collectively held.

  • Region of origin verified

    The workshop's location has been confirmed against the region the craft claims — the safeguard against "inspired by" imitations produced somewhere else entirely.

Two — recommendation logic

A cap you can see

Recommendations — in the gallery and in the AURA Studios feed — are weighted on completed purchases and high private craftsmanship ratings, never on views, watch-time or engagement. And every piece has a ceiling.

500

Daily sales, then rotation pauses

Once a piece reaches 500 completed sales in a day, its feed rotation is paused until the next day and the slot passes to an under-represented maker.

50

…or 50 high-rated allocations

A piece is also paused once it has been allocated to 50 highly-rated feed placements that day — whichever ceiling it hits first.

10★

Craftsmanship drives the feed

Vertical-feed placement is weighted heavily on high 10-star craftsmanship scores. Lower-scored pieces are suppressed from the feed regardless of how many views their video has.

When either ceiling is met, this exact card is replaced — in the grid and in the feed — by a piece from a maker with fewer sales and comparable 10-star ratings. The buyer sees no interruption, only a different, equally verified object.

Three — new artisans

A rotation of their own, with a guaranteed window

A maker who has just joined has no ratings, and an unrated piece can't compete on a ratings-driven feed. So new artisans aren't placed in the main selection at all — they appear in Newly Discovered Artisans, a separate rotating shelf of five to ten makers on the homepage and gallery.

On onboarding, every new artisan is also allocated a guaranteed baseline of 1,000 feed impressions in AURA Studios — real exposure before the algorithm has a single 10-star score to weigh them on. They keep that visibility until they've gathered enough ratings to hold their own, then graduate into the general pool. Being new is a starting line here, not a disadvantage.

Four — the rating system

A private 10-star evaluation, not a review wall

There are no public review threads on Provenance. After a piece is received, the buyer gives one private 10-star score built from three axes only — material quality, cultural authenticity, and craft execution. Nothing about shipping speed, packaging, or how "aesthetic" the unboxing was.

Scale
10 stars, post-delivery, one per acquisition
Assessed on
Material quality · cultural authenticity · craft execution
Who sees an individual score
The maker and Provenance. No one else.
What appears publicly
Nothing numeric. No average on the page, no counter, no review wall.
What the score does
Weights feed and gallery recommendations; low scores are suppressed from the vertical feed
What it is not
A popularity metric, a leaderboard, or a thing to farm

A score here is a signal to the system and a note to the maker — not a trophy on a shelf.
